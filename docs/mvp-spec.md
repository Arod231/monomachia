# Monomachia — MVP Plan & Spec

Sep 30, 2026 · @Andrew Rodriguez

The MVP is a playable browser demo of Monomachia's core duel: you against a computer opponent or a friend on the same screen, first to 3 rounds, with the posture, parry, disarm and ultimate loop from the design doc. It is built in three.js with simple shapes and code-generated sound, so it runs from a single file with nothing to install.

**Status, Sept 30, 2026:** all 12 milestones are done. Play the demo or open the standalone Monomachia.html file. The source is packaged for GitHub, with a README, tests and ready-made workflows.

### At a glance

The core loop · armed, posture full, disarmed

Pressure fills the other fighter's posture; once it is full, one parried attack sends their weapon flying until they retrieve it. At 25% HP or less, each fighter also gets one ultimate per round.

### Decisions in plain English

Every choice below trades polish for speed: the goal is to prove the fight feels right before spending on art, servers or content.

| Decision | What we chose | Why, in plain terms | What it costs us |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where it runs | A web browser, built with three.js (a free toolkit for drawing 3D in web pages) | Nothing to install. You open one file or a link, and changes are quick to try | Less graphical power than Unreal or Unity. The rules can move to a bigger engine later |
| How it looks | Fighters built from simple blocks and capsules, moved by code like puppets on strings | Proves the gameplay first. Real models and motion capture are the most expensive part and can be swapped in later without touching the rules | It looks like a prototype, not a finished game |
| Who you fight | A computer opponent with 3 difficulty levels, a training dummy, and a watch mode (computer vs computer) | You can play and test alone at any time | Online play waits: it needs servers and lag-hiding code, which is months of work |
| How many weapons | 3 of the 9: Katana (medium), Greatsword (colossal), Twin Daggers (small) | One from each size class shows the full fast-to-heavy range. Together they include all three unblockable types, so every counter can be tried | 6 weapons wait. Adding each is mostly filling in numbers and moves, not new rules |
| Camera | Always locked on to the opponent, behind your fighter's back | It is a 1-v-1 duel, and the design doc describes movement relative to the enemy. An on-screen marker points to a dropped weapon | No free-roaming camera yet |
| Controls | Keyboard and mouse, plus PlayStation and Xbox controllers. Fight sticks work through the D-pad. Every button can be remapped and is saved in the browser | Matches the design doc's custom-controls requirement and how fighting-game players actually play | Mappings stay in that browser; no online accounts |
| Game speed | The game thinks in a fixed 60 steps per second, like arcade fighters | Parry and dodge timing is identical on every computer, fast or slow | None worth noting |
| Sound | Generated by code: clangs, a special parry ring, slices, heavy crunches, and a drum-and-strings loop | No audio files to find or license | Less rich than recorded audio, which can replace it later |
| Bare-hand moves | One shared punch-and-kick set for all fighters | Saves a large amount of animation work | The design doc wants a different set per fighter; that comes later |
| Block abilities | Each weapon offers 3; you pick 2 before the fight (one on light, one on heavy) | Follows the design doc and shows the loadout idea with little extra work | A smaller pool than a full game would have |
| Two players | Added Versus: two players on one split screen. Each picks keyboard and mouse, an arrow-key layout, or a controller | Once the rules were separate from the screen, local head-to-head cost little, and it lets two people test the duel | Still no online play; two keyboard players share one keyboard |
| Posture tuning | Unguarded hits fill posture 1.5× the attack's value, a parry adds 16, and the meter waits 0.75 s before draining | Robot matches showed disarms were rare, 0.09 per round. They now happen about 0.45 times per round | First-pass numbers; your playtests should settle them |
| Safe graphics | If a device can't draw the full lighting, the game switches to simpler lighting by itself and says so | The first published build showed invisible fighters in the Claude app's browser. The game now recovers from that kind of failure on its own | Safe mode looks flatter |

### Controls

Defaults follow Souls-style habits; every action can be remapped on the Controls screen and is saved per profile.

| Action | Keyboard & mouse | PlayStation | Xbox | Fight stick preset |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Move (8 ways around the opponent) | W A S D | Left stick or D-pad | Left stick or D-pad | Stick (D-pad or left-stick mode) |
| Step | Tap a direction | Tap a direction | Tap a direction | Tap a direction |
| Sprint | Double-tap and hold a direction | Double-tap and hold, or L3 | Double-tap and hold, or LS click | Double-tap and hold, or L2 |
| Light attack | Left click (alt: J) | R1 | RB | □ |
| Heavy attack (hold to charge) | Right click (alt: K) | R2 | RT | △ |
| Block (hold) / Parry (tap on impact) | Shift (alt: L) | L1 | LB | R1 |
| Dodge (with a direction) / Backstep (no direction) | Space | ○ | B | ○ |
| Jump | F (alt: I) | ✕ | A | ✕ |
| Ultimate | Left + right click together (alt: Q or U) | △, or R1 + R2 | Y, or RB + RT | □ + △, or L1 |
| Block ability 1 / 2 | Hold block + light / heavy | Hold L1 + R1 / R2 | Hold LB + RB / RT | Hold R1 + □ / △ |
| Pick up weapon | E | □ | X | R2 |
| Pause | Esc or P | Options | Menu | Options |

PlayStation button names appear automatically when a PlayStation controller is detected. Controllers the browser does not recognise can still be set up by pressing each button on the Controls screen.

In Versus, each player picks a device and a controls profile. Two players can share one keyboard: player 2 uses the arrow-key layout (arrows move; J, K, L light, heavy, block; ; dodge; I jump; O pick up; U ultimate; Backspace pause; numpad 4 5 6 0 8 9 7 also work). With two controllers, each player keeps their own even if the other is unplugged.

### Combat rules

Each fighter has 100 HP and a 100-point posture meter; empty HP loses the round, and the first to win 3 rounds wins the match.

#### Attacks

| Attack | What it is | How to defend |
|---|---|---|
| Light | Fast strikes that chain 3–4 times | Block, parry or dodge |
| Heavy | Slower, more HP and posture damage, more knockback; chains with lights | Block, parry or dodge |
| Charged heavy | Hold heavy; it releases by itself after 2.5 s as a power attack, then leaves you open | Block (disarms a blocker whose posture is full), parry or dodge |
| Movement attacks | Different moves out of a sprint, a dodge, a backstep or a jump | Block, parry or dodge |
| Block abilities | Hold block and press light or heavy for the weapon's two chosen abilities | Depends on the ability |
| Thrust (unblockable) | Slow, telegraphed stab | Dodge into it for the Stomp counter (opponent stunned, open to a combo), or parry |
| Sweep (unblockable) | Slow, telegraphed swing at the feet | Jump over it for the Leap counter (vault off them for big posture damage), or parry |
| Overhead slam (unblockable) | Slow, telegraphed smash | Back-dash for the Evade counter, then a lunge strike while they recover, or parry |

Unblockables show a red 危 ("danger") mark and the attack type above the attacker during the wind-up. Dodge invincibility does not work against them, and trying to block one takes the full hit.

#### Defense

- **Block (hold):** no HP damage from blockable attacks, but reduced posture damage still lands (60–80% of the attack's posture value, by weapon). You must face the attacker.

- **Parry (tap block just before impact):** works on every attack. Window: Daggers 0.10 s, Katana 0.15 s, Greatsword 0.20 s. The attacker's weapon bounces off with a clang, they take 16 posture damage, and you act first; they can still parry your follow-up. Pressing block repeatedly shrinks the window, so mashing fails, and one press parries only one hit.

- **Dodge (direction + dodge):** a dash of about 3 m, invincible for roughly the first 0.2 s. Dodge with no direction is a backstep, which counts as a back-dash.

- **Jump:** clears sweeps and opens jumping attacks.

#### Posture

- Fills when you block (reduced), get hit (1.5× the attack's posture value), get parried (16) or get countered (15–35).

- Drains only while you hold block and have taken no posture damage for 0.75 s: about 14 points/s standing still, 5/s moving, at full HP. Lower HP slows it, down to 35% of normal near 0 HP.

- A full meter flashes. While it is full, a parried attack disarms you, and so does trying to block an unblockable, a full-charge power attack or an ultimate. The disarming blow does no HP damage.

#### Disarmed mode

- The weapon flies off and lands in the arena, marked on screen. Your posture resets to empty.

- You move 20% faster, dodge 50% farther and jump 35% higher.

- No block or parry. Tapping block on impact performs a Redirect counter: 35 posture damage and a stun; it disarms an armed attacker whose posture is full and dazes a bare-handed one for 1 s.

- Punches and kicks do little HP damage but high posture damage and extra knockback.

- Stand on your weapon and press Pick up to re-arm (about 0.4 s, and you are open meanwhile). The computer opponent tries to stand in your way.

#### Ultimates

- Unlocks at 25% HP or less, once per round; your fighter glows while it is ready.

- Press light and heavy together.

- Armed: the weapon's own ultimate (see Fighters). Disarmed: choose Recall (the weapon flies back to your hand) or Breaker Palm (a big posture blow).

#### Rounds

First to 3 round wins. HP, posture, weapons and ultimates reset every round. There is no round timer, and a double KO replays the round.

### Fighters

Three weapons, one per size class, cover the design doc's full range from fast-and-light to slow-and-crushing.

| Fighter | Class | Speed | HP damage | Posture damage | Reach | Parry window | Posture taken when blocking |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Katana | Medium | Medium | Medium | Medium | 2.2 m | 0.15 s | 70% |
| Greatsword | Colossal | Slow (moves 10% slower) | High | High | 3.0 m | 0.20 s | 60% |
| Twin Daggers | Small | Fast (moves 12% faster) | Good over many hits | Low | 1.8 m | 0.10 s | 80% |

#### Katana — balanced

- **Chains:** 3 lights (right cut, return cut, crown cut) and 2 heavies, freely mixed.

- **Block abilities (pick 2):** Flash, a parry stance with a wide window that stuns the attacker (default light slot); Piercing Thrust, unblockable (default heavy slot); Swallow Sweep, an unblockable low cut.

- **Ultimate — Moonsplitter:** sheathe, then tilt up/down for a vertical slash or left/right for a horizontal one that crosses the whole stage. Step aside from the vertical; jump the horizontal.

#### Greatsword — slow and crushing

- **Chains:** 2 lights and 2 heavies, all with big knockback and a bone-and-rock crunch on hit.

- **Block abilities (pick 2):** Reaping Sweep, unblockable (default light slot); Mountain Slam, an unblockable overhead (default heavy slot); Guard Crusher, a shoulder bash that deals heavy posture damage even when blocked.

- **Ultimate — Impaler:** take aim, dash across the stage and impale the opponent; press heavy while they are impaled to detonate a burst of energy.

#### Twin Daggers — fast and slippery

- **Chains:** 4 lights and 2 heavies; the longest dodges of any weapon.

- **Block abilities (pick 2):** Serpent Sweep, a low dashing unblockable sweep (default light slot); Shadow Step, a blink behind the opponent that turns your next light into a backstab (default heavy slot); Needle Thrust, a quick unblockable thrust.

- **Ultimate — Lightning Tempest:** dash like lightning to the opponent and spin through 6 strikes; each spin can be parried in turn.

#### Bare hands (every fighter, when disarmed)

A jab–cross–hook chain, roundhouse and spinning-heel kicks, a flying knee out of a sprint, and an axe kick from a jump.

### Computer opponent

The computer plays through a virtual controller, so it follows exactly your rules and timings; it cannot cheat, only react.

How it decides, several times a second:

- Holds its weapon's ideal distance: closes in, circles, or backs off.

- Attacks with a mix of chains, heavies, sprint attacks and unblockables (more often when you hide behind your block).

- Reacts to your attacks after a human-like delay: parry, block, dodge, or the correct counter for an unblockable.

- Blocks to drain its own posture when the meter runs high.

- When you are disarmed, it stands between you and your weapon. When it is disarmed, it runs for its weapon or fights bare-handed.

- Fires its ultimate when it unlocks and you are in range.

| Level | Reaction time | Parry chance | Correct-counter chance | Aggression |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Easy | 0.45 s | 8% | 10% | Low |
| Normal | 0.30 s | 30% | 35% | Medium |
| Hard | 0.18 s | 55% | 60% | High |

**Training dummy:** pick what it does — stand still, block, light chains, heavies, thrust, sweep, overhead slam, or random — with optional auto-refilling HP and posture. The screen says whether each parry was early or late and by how much, and names each counter as it lands.

**Watch mode:** two computer fighters duel, which is handy for learning the moves and for our automated tests.

### Look and sound

The mood is gritty dark fantasy in an ancient oriental setting, drawn with simple shapes and strong lighting.

- **Arena — Moonlit Shrine:** a round stone courtyard with cracked tiles, a torii gate, stone lanterns with flickering firelight, broken pillars, drifting embers and ash, fog and a blood-red moon.

- **Fighters:** armored figures built from simple shapes, with masks and sashes; you in crimson and black, the opponent in steel blue and black.

- **Readability effects:** weapon trails (white for normal attacks, red for unblockables, gold for ultimates), sparks on every clang, a bright ring on a parry, the red 危 mark on unblockables, a glowing aura when an ultimate is ready, and a marker on a dropped weapon.

- **Game feel:** a split-second freeze on hits (hit-stop), camera shake on heavy blows, slow motion on the final blow.

- **HUD:** classic fighting-game health bars at the top with the posture bar under each, round pips and an ultimate-ready badge; big center text for Round, Fight, Parry, Disarmed and K.O.

- **Sound, all generated by code:** a metal clang on blocks, a brighter ringing clang unique to parries, slicing hits for blades, bone-and-rock crunches for the Greatsword, whooshes on swings and dodges, taiko hits for round calls, and a drums, plucked strings and distorted guitar music loop. Volume and mute sit in the pause menu.

### Gaps in the design doc

These 13 points were open or ambiguous in the design doc; the demo uses the answers below, and each is easy to change if you read it differently.

| The design doc says | Open question | What the demo does |
|---|---|---|
| "each attack will increase their posture meter" | The attacker's meter or the defender's? | The defender's. Pressure fills the other fighter's meter, and a full meter is dangerous |
| "A player doesn't receive damage to their HP when they're disarmed" | Are disarmed fighters immune to damage? | No. Only the blow that disarms deals no HP damage; otherwise a disarmed fighter could never lose |
| Nothing on posture after a disarm | Does the meter stay full? | It resets to empty. While disarmed it drains slowly by itself, since you can't block; if it fills again you are dazed for a second |
| "a power attack" | Which attacks count? | A heavy attack charged for the full 2.5 s |
| Blocking "an ultimate ability" | Can ultimates be blocked at all? | Yes, at a heavy posture cost, except the Katana and Greatsword ultimates, which are unblockable |
| Disarmed ultimate: "re-arm ... or deal a hard blow" | How does the player choose? | Light + heavy opens a split-second choice: light recalls the weapon, heavy throws the Breaker Palm. No choice means Recall |
| Parry on a precise tap | What stops players mashing block? | Each extra press within half a second shrinks the parry window |
| Disarmed counter "replaces the parry" | Can a disarmed fighter disarm back? | Yes. A Redirect counter against a full-posture attacker disarms them, and both fight bare-handed |
| No mention of a clock | Is there a round timer? | No timer. A double KO replays the round |
| Parrier "can follow up" | Who acts first after a parry? | The parried attacker is stuck in a short recoil, so the parrier strikes first; that strike can still be parried |
| Unblockables are "telegraphed" | How does the player know which counter to use? | A red 危 mark plus the attack type (THRUST, SWEEP or SLAM) over the attacker |
| Katana: only "Flash" is named | What are its other block abilities? | Added Piercing Thrust and Swallow Sweep as the other two options |
| Thrusts and sweeps "cannot be ... dodged" | Can you simply move out of the way? | Dodge invincibility doesn't protect you, but getting fully out of range still works; thrusts turn to track you during the wind-up |

### Testing

Each milestone is checked by automated tests first and by eye second.

- **Rule tests:** checks that run the combat rules without graphics, such as "a parry adds 16 posture to the attacker" or "blocking a sweep with a full meter disarms".

- **Robot matches:** computer-vs-computer matches at high speed. They must finish without errors, and parries, counters, disarms and ultimates must all occur.

- **Screen checks:** a hidden browser loads the game, plays scripted inputs and takes screenshots, which I review for visual problems.

- **Balance numbers:** the robot matches report average round length and how often each event happens, so tuning rests on data.

- **Your playtest:** the last check is you playing it; the Gaps table lists the rules to confirm.

**Latest results:** 47 rule tests pass, 6 of them guarding against bugs an independent code review found. 40 robot matches (149 rounds) finished with no errors, averaging 44 s a round and 0.45 disarms a round, and each weapon won 48–52% of its matches. The browser checks drew every screen with no graphics errors, and a deliberately forced graphics failure recovered by itself. A fresh install from the packaged source built the same file byte for byte.

### Known limitations

- No online play; Versus is two players on one screen.

- The Claude app's built-in viewer may block controllers. Open the game in its own browser tab, or open the saved file, to use one.

- On a device that can't draw the full lighting, the game switches to safe graphics by itself. It looks flatter.

- Split screen skips the glow effect so two views stay smooth.

- Balance numbers come from robot matches; your playtests should drive the next round of tuning.

- Control profiles and settings live in one browser; clearing site data resets them.
