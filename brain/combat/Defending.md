# Defending

- **[[Block]] (hold):** blockable attacks do no HP damage, but reduced posture damage still lands (60–80% of the attack's posture value, depending on the weapon). The blocker must face the attacker. A blocking fighter walks at 60% of running speed (it was 45%).
- **[[Parry]] (tap block just before impact):** works on every attack. The window is 0.10 s for the Daggers, 0.15 s for the Katana and 0.20 s for the Greatsword. The attacker's weapon bounces off, they take 16 posture damage, and the parrier acts first, though their follow-up can be parried in turn. Mashing block shrinks the window, and one press parries only one hit. The rebuild keeps the rules but makes it cinematic, as in Sekiro: both weapons rebound from the [[Contact point]], with sparks, a ring, a clang and a brief camera push-in.
- **Dodge (direction + dodge):** a dash of about 3 m, invincible for roughly the first 0.2 s. A dash, not a roll. Dodge with no direction is a backstep, which counts as a back-dash.
- **Jump:** clears sweeps and opens jumping attacks.
- **[[Flash]]:** the Katana's parry stance with a wide window that stuns.
- **Disarmed:** no block or parry; the timed [[Redirect]] takes their place. See [[Hand-to-hand]].

[[Unblockable|Unblockables]] go through all of this. Each has its own counter: see [[Unblockables and counters]]. A parry fills the attacker's posture and a block fills your own, which is the heart of the [[Posture system]].
